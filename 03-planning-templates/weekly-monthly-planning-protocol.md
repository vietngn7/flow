# Weekly & Monthly Planning Protocol

Evidence-based system for coordination across time horizons.

---

## How this works

**Three planning levels, three different questions:**

- **Monthly:** Why am I doing this? (strategy, values, direction)
- **Weekly:** How will I do this? (feasibility, resources, implementation)  
- **Daily:** What exactly, when exactly? (specific tasks, timing, sequences)

*Temporal construal theory: distant future = abstract thinking, near future = concrete thinking.*

---

# Daily Integration

Your daily planning feeds into weekly, which feeds into monthly.

## Daily → Weekly connection

**At end of each day, update your weekly tracker:**

☐ Track actual time spent on weekly priorities  
☐ Note progress made (even small)  
☐ Record any obstacles encountered  
☐ Update completion percentages

*This 2-minute daily update gives you objective data for weekly reviews.*

---

## Weekly → Monthly connection

**During weekly review, check monthly progress:**

☐ Update monthly goal metrics  
☐ Calculate percentage complete  
☐ Assess if you're on track (if 25% through month, are you 25% toward goal?)  
☐ Adjust weekly plans if falling behind

*Rate-of-progress monitoring generates affect. Slow progress triggers effort increase; fast progress can trigger complacency.*

## Hierarchy of planning

```
MONTHLY (Abstract)           WEEKLY (Medium)              DAILY (Concrete)
     ↓                            ↓                           ↓
"Why am I doing this?"      "How will I do it?"      "What exactly, when?"
Strategy + Values        Resources + Feasibility    Tasks + Timing
Learning goals           Subgoals + milestones      Specific actions
```

*Each level operates at different construal level (Temporal Construal Theory). Translation between levels requires implementation intentions.*

# When Plans Fail

## If you consistently miss weekly targets:

1. ☐ **Check buffer:** Increase time estimates by 15-25%
2. ☐ **Check goal type:** Switch performance goals to learning goals for complex tasks
3. ☐ **Check obstacles:** Add more if-then plans for disruptions
4. ☐ **Check workload:** You might be planning for ideal week, not realistic week

## If you skip reviews:

1. ☐ **Strengthen implementation intention:** More specific day/time/location
2. ☐ **Add accountability:** Share commitment with someone
3. ☐ **Reduce scope:** Try 10-minute review instead of 20-minute
4. ☐ **Change timing:** Morning vs. evening, or try biweekly instead of weekly

## If monthly goals feel overwhelming:

1. ☐ **Too many goals:** Cut to 3 maximum
2. ☐ **Wrong goal type:** Switch to learning goals if too complex
3. ☐ **Too abstract:** Break into smaller weekly milestones
4. ☐ **Misaligned with daily:** Add implementation intentions connecting monthly to daily

*Feedback loops require adjustment. No system works perfectly for everyone.*

# Evidence appendix

## What's proven vs. what's not

### HIGH CONFIDENCE (meta-analytic support, large samples)

**Implementation intentions work**
- Effect size: d = 0.65-0.66
- 642 independent tests (2024 meta-analysis update)
- Must be contingent if-then format
- Requires rehearsal at least once
- Public commitment enhances effects

**Progress monitoring works**
- Effect size: d = 0.40 (95% CI [0.32, 0.48])
- 138 randomized controlled trials, 19,951 participants
- Physical recording >> mental tracking
- Public reporting >> private tracking
- Objective metrics >> subjective impressions

**Specific difficult goals outperform vague goals**
- Effect size: d = 0.42-0.80
- 400+ studies across 35 years (Locke & Latham)
- 40,000+ participants
- Learning goals better for complex novel tasks

**Small wins drive motivation**
- 12,000 daily diary entries (Amabile)
- Progress = strongest predictor of positive emotions
- Setbacks carry 2-3× stronger negative impact
- Managers underestimate: only 5% ranked progress as top motivator

**Planning fallacy is universal**
- Only 30-45% finish in predicted time
- Underestimate by 20-50% across all time scales
- Correction: recall past similar tasks + explicit linking
- Building 25-50% buffers recommended

**WOOP (mental contrasting + implementation intentions)**
- Effect size: g = 0.336
- 21 studies, ~16,000 participants
- Face-to-face: g = 0.465
- Document-based: g = 0.277

**Time management correlates with wellbeing**
- Life satisfaction: r = .47 (158 studies, 53,957 participants)
- Job performance: r = .25
- Psychological distress: r = -.36
- Largely dispositional (conscientiousness r = .451)

### MODERATE CONFIDENCE (good evidence, fewer studies)

**Weekly or biweekly cycles**
- One field study: 7-8% improvement (N=9,108)
- Biweekly slightly more sustainable than weekly
- No experimental comparison of weekly vs. other frequencies
- Theoretical support from proximal goals research

**Proximal subgoals + distal goals = optimal**
- 57.1% completion (hybrid) vs. 39.1% (subgoals only) vs. 33.8% (goal only)
- N=207 (Huang, Jin, & Zhang)
- Subgoals without direction fragment motivation

**Coping planning for maintenance**
- φ = .38-.41 correlation with behavior change
- Action planning for initiation, coping planning for obstacles
- Contingent planning buffers interruption effects

**Future self-continuity predicts decisions**
- Neural similarity (current self → future self) predicts patience
- Greater continuity → more accumulated assets
- Making consequences vivid improves long-term choices

### WEAK CONFIDENCE (theoretical only, untested, or single studies)

**Weekly reviews specifically**
- NO experimental validation of weekly as optimal frequency
- Ubiquitous in productivity systems but unsupported by RCTs
- May work through progress monitoring (d=0.40) but frequency unspecified

**Time blocking**
- ZERO peer-reviewed empirical support
- Searches found no published experiments in academic journals
- Popular practitioner advice, no scientific validation

**Monthly planning advantages**
- No studies comparing monthly cycles to other intervals
- Theoretical: Monthly might activate abstract thinking (values, strategy)
- Empirically: Untested

**Exploration vs. exploitation balance**
- Theoretical framework exists from behavioral ecology
- NO quantified optimal ratios for knowledge workers
- No studies testing whether weekly planning enables better balance

**Schedule granularity**
- Hour-by-hour vs. daily themes vs. weekly blocks: Unstudied
- No performance comparisons exist

### NOT SUPPORTED (failed replication or negative evidence)

**Ego depletion**
- Originally claimed: d = 0.62 (198 studies)
- Major replication failed to find effect
- Extensive publication bias
- True effect likely near zero
- Concerns about "too much planning causing depletion" unfounded

**Weekly reviews as uniquely optimal**
- Biweekly showed equal effectiveness in one direct comparison
- Weekly may not be special frequency

**Multitasking reduction as primary mechanism**
- Time management → reduced multitasking: r = -.09 (weak)
- Time management works through other mechanisms (prioritization, commitment)

## Key meta-analyses cited

1. **Implementation intentions:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) - 94 studies; Updated (2024) - 642 tests
2. **Progress monitoring:** Harkin et al. (2016) - 138 RCTs, 19,951 participants  
3. **Goal-setting theory:** Locke & Latham (2002) - 400+ studies, 40,000+ participants
4. **Time management:** Aeon, Faber, & Panaccio (2021) - 158 studies, 53,957 participants
5. **WOOP (mental contrasting):** Oettingen et al. (2021) - 21 studies, ~16,000 participants
6. **Planning fallacy:** Buehler, Griffin, & Ross (1994) - Multiple studies documenting 30-45% completion rates

---

## What research is missing

**Critical gaps requiring future research:**

- Direct experimental comparison of weekly vs. biweekly vs. monthly planning formats
- Weekly review effectiveness validation (ubiquitous recommendation, zero RCT support)
- Time blocking effectiveness (zero published studies despite widespread promotion)
- Optimal time allocation ratios (exploration/exploitation, strategic/operational)
- Schedule granularity effects on performance
- Cultural and individual difference moderators
- Longitudinal compound effects over years
- Review mechanism decomposition (retrospection vs. prospection vs. adjustment)

*Popular productivity advice often runs ahead of scientific evidence. Use proven mechanisms (implementation intentions, progress monitoring) rather than adopting entire unvalidated systems.*

## Remember

**Three horizons, three questions:**
- Monthly asks "Why?" — Strategy, values, direction
- Weekly asks "How?" — Feasibility, resources, implementation  
- Daily asks "What/When?" — Specific tasks, timing, sequences

**Track objectively, not subjectively:**
- Write down metrics
- Use actual data in reviews
- Physical recording beats mental tracking

**Build buffers for reality:**
- 25-50% time buffers for most people
- Your personal planning fallacy rate varies
- Track and adjust

**Celebrate progress explicitly:**
- Small wins drive motivation more than anything
- Setbacks teach 2-3× more but don't dwell disproportionately
- Write progress down during reviews

**Connect levels with if-then plans:**
- Implementation intentions bridge abstract to concrete
- Specify when, where, how
- Rehearse at least once

**No system is perfect:**
- Weekly might not be uniquely optimal (biweekly may work equally well)
- Individual differences matter (conscientiousness r=.451)
- Adjust based on your own data
