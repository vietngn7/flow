## Quick tips

### Starting is hard?

**Use if-then planning.** It works.

*When [specific moment], I'll immediately [first action].*

Examples:
- *When I close Slack at 9:00 AM, I'll immediately open my report and write the first sentence.*
- *When I finish lunch at 1:00 PM, I'll immediately open the code editor and run the test suite.*
- *When my 3:00 PM meeting ends, I'll immediately call mom while walking.*

**→** *Detailed planner? Use all steps. Simple thinker? Pick your top 3.*

### Can't focus?

**Turn off all notifications during focus time.**

Every ping costs you 23 minutes of focus.

Try:
- Phone on airplane mode
- Slack on Do Not Disturb  
- Email closed entirely
- 40-90 minute focus blocks

### Feeling overwhelmed?

**You can only do 3-6 hours of quality thinking per day.**

Stop trying to do more.

**Targets are for focus, not pressure.**  
Aim for 15, complete 10? That's progress. That's a win.

### Procrastinating?

**Strengthen your if-then plan.**

Vague: "I'll work on the report today"  
Strong: "When I close Slack at 9 AM, I'll immediately open the report and write one sentence"

The more specific the trigger, the less willpower required.

### Notifications keep interrupting?

**Create do-not-disturb blocks.**

Even 2 hours of protected time beats 8 hours of constant interruptions.

Schedule:
- Morning deep work: 9-11 AM
- Afternoon focus: 2-4 PM
- All notifications off during these blocks

### Energy crashes in afternoon?

**Schedule your hardest work for morning.**

Cognitive performance peaks 2-4 hours after waking.

Save meetings, admin work, and lighter tasks for afternoon when energy naturally dips.

### Can't stop thinking about work at night?

**Write it down before leaving.**

Your brain keeps reminding you because it's afraid you'll forget.

Brain dump → specific plan → mental closure.

### Tasks feel too big?

**Break them smaller.**

Can't start "Write report"?  
Try "Write one sentence of introduction"

Momentum builds. Starting is everything.

## Common myths that waste your time

**❌ Multitasking = productivity**  
*"Handle email while in meetings."*

Opposite. Task switching costs 23 minutes per interruption. Do one thing well.

---

**❌ Long hours = better results**  
*"Stay late to get more done."*

False. Quality drops after 3-6 hours of intense work. Breaks improve performance.

---

**❌ Checking email constantly = responsiveness**  
*"I need to be always available."*

Wrong. Batch email checking 2-3x daily. Constant checking destroys focus and adds stress.

---

**❌ Finishing everything = success**  
*"I must complete my entire list."*

Unrealistic. Progress on important work > completing everything. Prioritize ruthlessly.

---

**❌ Breaks = wasted time**  
*"I'm too busy to take breaks."*

Backwards. Strategic breaks improve performance more than working through. You're too busy NOT to break.

---

**❌ Scrolling = relaxation**  
*"I scroll social media to decompress."*

Incorrect. Brain scans show sustained arousal, not rest. Walking restores better than scrolling.

---

**❌ More hours = more output**  
*"Work longer to achieve more."*

Opposite. 3-6 hours of quality focus beats 12 hours of fragmented work.

## Why this works

### The science, simplified

**If-then planning (Implementation Intentions)**
- Increases goal completion from 22% to 62%
- Creates automatic behavior triggers
- Removes decision fatigue
- 94 studies, consistent results

**Daily progress on meaningful work**
- #1 factor for motivation (Amabile, 12,000 diary entries)
- Small wins create momentum
- More important than task completion
- Focus on progress, not perfection

**Goal types for different tasks**
- Simple execution tasks: specific targets work best (d=0.67)
- Complex problem-solving: learning goals often better (d=0.48)
- Known process example: "15 code reviews by 3 PM"
- Figuring out example: "Understand 3 different API design approaches"
- Locke & Latham (2002): Match goal type to task complexity
- Wrong goal type can reduce effectiveness 30-40%
- Targets provide focus and direction, not pressure
- Progress toward goal = success, not just achievement

**Social connection**
- Largest effect size: 50% increase in survival
- Same impact as quitting smoking
- Face-to-face > video > text
- 10 minutes daily minimum

**Physical activity**
- 150 minutes/week minimum
- Improves cognitive function (large effect)
- Increases BDNF (brain growth factor)
- Works within hours, compounds over time

**Learning new skills**
- Medium-to-large cognitive benefits
- Must be real skills, not brain games
- 30-60 minutes daily
- Variety + social component optimal

**Screen management**
- >3 hours/day recreational = 28% cognitive decline
- Notifications destroy focus (23-minute recovery)
- Night screens harm sleep quality
- Digital detox shows benefits within days

**Clear plans reduce stress**
- Brain needs "how" not "done"
- Planning reduces stress as much as completion
- Unfinished + planned = no mental tension
- Trying to finish everything backfires

**Interruption planning (Contingent Planning)**
- Traditional planning fails on chaotic days
- Backup plans protect progress
- Flexibility beats rigid schedules
- Works when interruptions are high

**Micro-breaks and recovery**
- 10-20 minute breaks: vigor +36%, fatigue -35%
- Early breaks recover more than late breaks
- Physical activity breaks most effective
- Evening recovery predicts next-day performance

**Psychological detachment**
- Strongest predictor against exhaustion
- Mental disengagement from work during non-work hours
- Reduces burnout, improves sleep, increases next-day performance
- Requires active practice, not just physical separation
- Evening recovery builds resources for next day

**Recovery activities effectiveness**
- Nature exposure: d=0.54-1.0 for working memory
- Physical movement: reduces neural activation while improving performance
- Social media scrolling: maintains cognitive arousal 15+ minutes after use
- Walking beats scrolling for genuine restoration
- Matching activity to energy level optimizes recovery

### Effect sizes compared

From strongest to weakest scientific evidence:

1. **Social connections**: 50% survival increase (huge)
2. **Physical activity**: 0.24-1.09 cognitive improvement (large)
3. **Daily progress**: Most important for motivation (proven)
4. **Psychological detachment**: Strongest predictor against exhaustion
5. **Micro-breaks**: 0.36 vigor improvement (medium)
6. **Learning skills**: Medium-large cognitive effects
7. **Screen management**: 28% cognitive decline if excessive
8. **Meditation**: 0.15-0.35 cognitive improvement (small-medium)

**Debunked concepts** (effect size ≈ 0):
- Ego depletion: 0.04-0.06 (essentially zero)
- Zeigarnik effect: 0.15 (doesn't replicate reliably)
- Brain training games: No transfer to real skills
- Social media as recovery: Negative effects on restoration
